흥부와 놀부 삽화 프롬프트

24쪽 · 그림체와 인물 설정을 앞에 한 번만 두고 SECTION 1~24로 이어 붙였습니다. 한 번에 맡기시면 됩니다.

Draw 24 illustrations for a Korean children's picture book: HEUNGBU AND NOLBU.
Each SECTION below is one page. Keep the style and the character designs identical across all 24.

STYLE (apply identically to every section): 3D CGI children's picture-book render, Pixar/Disney-like soft shading. Chubby rounded characters with oversized heads, large glossy eyes, rosy cheeks, tiny noses. Traditional Korean village setting — thatched-roof cottages (choga-jip), tiled-roof hanok, stone walls, dirt paths, rolling green hills, soft clouds. Warm cinematic lighting, gentle depth of field, clean bright colors. Aspect ratio 4:3, 1184x864.

CAST (keep these designs identical in every section):
- HEUNGBU — younger brother. Cream/beige hanbok jacket with a visible cloth patch, dark trousers, hair in a small top-knot, NO hat. Round friendly face, warm open smile.
- NOLBU — older brother. Deep blue hanbok jacket, black horsehair hat (gat) with chin strap, wide belt with a round decorative medallion. Same chubby build as Heungbu but chin raised, smug.
- HEUNGBU'S WIFE — pale grey-green hanbok, hair in a low bun. Kind tired face.
- NOLBU'S WIFE — pink or lavender hanbok, hair in a neat bun. Sharp pinched expression.
- CHILDREN — four small children of Heungbu, same rounded style, plain beige/grey hanbok, top-knots or small buns. Nolbu has two children in nicer clothes.
- SWALLOW — blue-black back, cream belly, red-orange face patch, small and round.
- DOKKAEBI — Korean goblins. Dark blue-grey skin, one horn, round yellow eyes, wild hair, tiger-skin wrap, wooden club. Comic and lively, never frightening.

RULES for every section:
(1) NO text anywhere — no letters, no Korean or Chinese characters, no numbers, no speech bubbles, no signature, no watermark.
(2) Never draw the same character twice in one image. No duplicate figures.
(3) The art must bleed to all four edges — no white margin, no rounded frame, no border.
(4) The lower quarter must be fully painted, never left blank.
(5) Wholesome, innocent, fully clothed, child-friendly. No blood, no wounds, no cruelty — even in the snake and goblin scenes.
(6) Only the characters named in that section appear. Do not add villagers or extra people.

SECTION 1
A wide Korean mountain village in daytime. LEFT: a fine tiled-roof hanok behind a stone wall — NOLBU and his wife stand in front of it, chins slightly raised, smug. RIGHT: a modest thatched-roof cottage — HEUNGBU, his wife and four small children stand in front of it waving cheerfully with both arms. A dirt path runs between the two houses. Rolling green hills behind.

SECTION 2
The courtyard of the tiled-roof hanok. NOLBU stands with one arm thrown out, pointing away, mouth open shouting, face angry. HEUNGBU stands with his head lowered, holding two of his children by the hand; his wife carries a cloth bundle of belongings. The children look up at their father, worried. Nolbu's wife watches from the doorway with folded arms.

SECTION 3
A tiny broken-down thatched cottage at the foot of a hill at dusk. The thatch is thin and patchy, a paper door is torn. Through the open door we see the single small room where HEUNGBU, his wife and four children huddle close together in a circle. Warm dim lamplight inside, cool blue evening outside.

SECTION 4
Inside the small thatched room at night, one oil lamp glowing. HEUNGBU sits cross-legged, hands raised mid-gesture as he tells a story, laughing warmly. His four children sit around him leaning in, eyes wide and sparkling. His wife sews in the corner, smiling. Cozy golden light.

SECTION 5
Inside the bare room in the morning. HEUNGBU'S WIFE holds an empty rice jar tipped over to show it is empty, her face sad. HEUNGBU sits still, looking down at his own hands, thinking hard. A worn overcoat hangs on the wall behind him. The four children sleep in a row under one thin blanket.

SECTION 6
At the gate of the tiled-roof hanok. NOLBU'S WIFE stands on the raised wooden floor holding a wooden rice paddle, arm swung through, face sharp and angry, a steaming rice pot beside her. HEUNGBU stands below on the ground, head turned by the blow, one cheek pink, both hands together pleading. A few grains of rice fly through the air.

SECTION 7
Same gate. HEUNGBU turns his OTHER cheek toward NOLBU'S WIFE and points at it with one finger, eyes bright and eager — not sad, almost delighted. Grains of cooked rice stick to his first cheek. Nolbu's wife recoils half a step, paddle still raised, utterly baffled. Heungbu's other hand is cupped, carefully holding rice grains.

SECTION 8
Spring. Under the eaves of the thatched cottage a pair of SWALLOWS build a small mud nest. HEUNGBU stands below looking up with a warm welcoming smile, one hand raised in greeting. His wife and children watch happily beside him. Pale green new leaves, soft blue sky.

SECTION 9
Close view of the nest under the eaves. Three baby swallows with wide-open yellow beaks chirp from the nest, fluffy and round. Below, HEUNGBU'S four children stand on tiptoe waving up at them, laughing. Bright warm daylight, a few flower petals drifting.

SECTION 10
Tense moment. A large snake climbs the stone wall and stretches slowly toward the swallow nest under the eaves, tongue flicking. The baby swallows huddle deep in the nest, trembling, tiny eyes wide. Cooler light and longer shadows, but gentle — not gruesome. No people in frame.

SECTION 11
One baby swallow has tumbled out of the nest and lies on the dirt ground, one leg bent, beak open crying. The other babies peer down from the nest above. HEUNGBU runs in from the side, arms reaching out, alarmed, hanbok fluttering.

SECTION 12
HEUNGBU kneels on the ground, cradling the baby swallow in one hand and carefully winding thin white silk thread around its tiny leg with the other. His face is gentle and focused, mouth in a soft 'oh'. The bird looks up at him trustingly. Tiled eave above, green hills behind.

SECTION 13
Autumn. The healed baby swallow flutters just above the courtyard testing its wings, a thin bandage still on one leg. HEUNGBU'S four children hold out millet in their open palms, cheering it on. Golden autumn grass, reddening leaves, a flock of swallows gathering high in the sky above the roof.

SECTION 14
Spring again. The swallow has landed on HEUNGBU'S open palm and is dropping a single small gourd seed into it. Heungbu stares at the seed, eyes huge with wonder, mouth open. The eaves of the thatched cottage above, new green leaves, warm morning light.

SECTION 15
Autumn. The thatched cottage is covered by an enormous gourd vine that has climbed all over the roof. Three gourds, each as big as a person, hang heavily from the roof. HEUNGBU and his family stand below looking up, mouths open in amazement, small beside the giant gourds.

SECTION 16
The whole family saws a giant gourd with a long two-handled saw, singing — HEUNGBU and his wife pull each side, the children push and cheer. The gourd has just split open and a mountain of white rice pours out onto the ground, spilling everywhere. Every face lit with astonishment and joy.

SECTION 17
The second and third gourds are open. Gold coins, jewels and bolts of silk spill out in a glittering pile; behind them a brand-new tiled-roof house stands where the thatched cottage was. HEUNGBU and his whole family hold hands in a ring, dancing, faces beaming. Sparkles in the air.

SECTION 18
NOLBU stands in his own courtyard, arms crossed, glaring off toward Heungbu's new tiled house visible in the distance. His face is twisted with envy, one eyebrow raised, teeth gritted. His wife whispers behind her hand beside him. Overcast, slightly cool light.

SECTION 19
NOLBU holds a swallow in both hands under his eaves and is wrapping its leg with a strip of cloth, grinning a sly crooked grin. The swallow's eyes are wide, beak open. Strictly non-graphic — show only the bandage and the bird's frightened face, no blood and no injury. Nolbu in his blue hanbok and black gat.

SECTION 20
Spring. NOLBU crouches beside a freshly planted mound in his courtyard, rubbing his hands together greedily, eyes gleaming. A swallow flies away in the background. His wife leans over his shoulder, equally eager. Bright, slightly harsh daylight.

SECTION 21
NOLBU and his wife saw open a giant gourd, both leaning back mid-pull with excited grins — and DOKKAEBI come pouring out of the split, clubs in hand. Nolbu's grin is freezing into shock, his gat tipping off. Comic and lively, not scary.

SECTION 22
The dokkaebi swarm the courtyard swinging their wooden clubs, laughing. NOLBU and his wife run in a circle around the yard, arms flailing, his gat flying off, comically panicked. Dust puffs and scattered pots. Playful slapstick — nobody is actually struck.

SECTION 23
The tiled-roof house is GONE — only bare ground and a stone foundation remain. The storehouse doors hang open and empty. NOLBU sits slumped alone on the empty ground, shoulders down, face crumpled. A single swallow flies past in the foreground. Grey overcast sky, muted colors, quiet.

SECTION 24
Sunset. HEUNGBU has both hands clasped around NOLBU'S hands. Nolbu's head is bowed, eyes wet; Heungbu looks at him warmly, smiling. Behind them both families stand together — both wives and all the children — watching with soft smiles. Two swallows fly side by side toward the roof above. Warm golden-pink sunset light.
