Draw 22 illustrations for a Korean children's picture book: CINDERELLA.
Each SECTION below is one page. Keep the style and the character designs identical across all 22.
Every image is landscape 4:3, 1184x864. Put NO words, letters, numbers or speech bubbles anywhere in any image.

STYLE (apply identically to every section): THE TWO THINGS THAT MATTER MOST, before anything else. (1) FACES: every face is a children's-picture-book face — a big round head, full round cheeks that push the eyes into soft curves, a tiny simple nose, large dark dot eyes with one white highlight, and a small curved mouth. Heads are drawn LARGE relative to the body, about one quarter of the total height. Never a small realistic face, never flat blank features, never a doll face, never an adult face on a child. (2) INK LINE: every shape is closed with a CLEARLY VISIBLE brush-drawn outline in warm soot-brown — a confident line that swells and tapers, a little dry and broken at its ends. The line must read plainly at a glance. Never a faint pencil line, never an outline that melts into the colour, never a soft airbrushed edge. Then the rest: transparent watercolour on cream paper, matte, the paper tooth breathing through every wash. Colour is laid broad and flat, two or three tones to an object, always kept inside the ink line. Keep it simple — a few big shapes make the picture and the rest of the frame stays calm. Pigments: warm ochre, raw sienna and soot grey for the kitchen and hearth; cerulean and pale indigo for night; rose, buttercup yellow and plum for the sisters and stepmother; chalk white with a faint silver-blue for the ball gown; gold ochre for the coach and candlelight. Even the night pages stay luminous. Flat hand-drawn 2D — NOT 3D, not CGI, not a glossy render, not anime or manga, not a Disney film still, not a muted flat-vector or mid-century poster look, no sparkles and no digital glow.

SETTING (the same world in every section): THE HOUSE is a comfortable old townhouse — panelled rooms, a staircase with a turned wooden banister, tall windows; THE KITCHEN at the back is plainer, with a big brick hearth, a low stool beside it, a scrubbed wooden table, hanging copper pans and a mouse hole in the skirting. THE PALACE is bright pale stone with blue-grey pointed roofs and a wide flight of steps; the BALLROOM has a chequered marble floor, tall arched windows and warm chandeliers — grand but simple. THE GARDEN has clipped hedges, a low stone balustrade and a fountain under a big moon. European storybook clothes of no particular century. Nothing modern anywhere

CAST (keep these designs identical in every section):
- CINDERELLA — a girl of about sixteen but drawn young and soft: round gentle face, warm ivory skin, pink cheeks, large dark dot eyes, honey-blonde hair. She has THREE looks that must never be mixed up. (a) CINDER LOOK (pages 1-9 and 19-21): faded brown work dress, grey apron, a cloth tied over her hair with strands escaping, bare feet or worn shoes, a light smudge of ash on one cheek. (b) BALL LOOK (pages 12-17): a flowing white gown with the faintest silver-blue sheen, hair swept up with a small white flower. The glass slippers are on her feet from page 13 onward — on page 12 they are still on the ground in front of her and her feet are bare. (c) WEDDING LOOK (page 22 only): a simple white wedding gown with a short veil. Her face and hair colour stay identical in all three
- THE STEPMOTHER — a well-dressed woman of about forty-five with a narrow but ordinary face, dark hair in a tight roll, a deep plum dress with a high collar and a brooch. She is COLD, not wicked-looking: no hooked nose, no pointed chin, no black cloak, no glaring eyes. She simply never smiles at Cinderella
- THE ELDER SISTER — tall and thin, red hair in tight ringlets, always in rose pink. Vain and fussy, drawn comically, never ugly and never cruel-faced
- THE YOUNGER SISTER — short and round, brown hair in a high bun, always in buttercup yellow. Vain and fussy, drawn comically, never ugly and never cruel-faced
- THE FAIRY GODMOTHER — a plump twinkling old woman with a round rosy face, white hair in a soft bun, round spectacles, a pale lilac cloak over a cream dress, holding a slim wand with a small star. Warm and grandmotherly; her magic shows as a soft warm glow and a few simple curved light lines, never glitter or beams
- THE PRINCE — a young man of about twenty with a round friendly face, dark wavy hair, warm skin, a cornflower-blue coat with simple gold trim and white breeches. Kind and a little shy, never haughty
- THE HERALD — a middle-aged man in a red coat with a scroll, later carrying a small velvet cushion
- THE FOUR MICE — four small grey mice, each with one telling difference (a white patch on the back, a notched ear, a plumper one, a longer tail) so they stay the same four all book
- THE GLASS SLIPPERS — a pair of clear, faintly blue-tinted slippers with a low curved heel and a small bow at the toe; identical every time they appear
- THE TWO SMALL BIRDS — two little round songbirds, one soft blue with a cream breast and one warm brown with a pale throat, both with tiny black dot eyes and short beaks. They are the same two birds every time they appear
