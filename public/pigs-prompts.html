18쪽 · 과슈 그림책 · 1184×864 (4:3). 한 덩어리로 주고 SECTION 을 그쪽에서 알아서 나누게 합니다.

Draw 18 illustrations for a Korean children's picture book: THE THREE LITTLE PIGS (아기 돼지 삼형제). Each SECTION below is one page of the book. Generate all 18, one image per SECTION, and deliver them separately in order.
Keep the style and the character designs IDENTICAL across all 18 images.

CANVAS (every image, no exceptions): 1184 x 864 pixels, 4:3 landscape. The art must bleed to all four edges — no white margin, no rounded frame, no border, no letterboxing. Do not add padding bars of any colour.

STYLE (apply identically to every section): GOUACHE picture-book painting. Opaque, matte paint with visible flat brush strokes and soft dry-brush edges. Bold simple shapes, confident dark outlines that vary in thickness. Slightly retro mid-century storybook feel. Warm limited palette — straw yellow, brick terracotta, moss green, cream, warm grey, a deep teal for night. Skies are washed flat, not photographic. No gradient meshes, no 3D rendering, no photo texture, no digital gloss.

CAST (keep these designs identical in every section — the three brothers must be told apart at a glance by colour and size):
- MOTHER PIG — large, soft pink pig, cream apron with a small patch, headscarf in muted rose, kind round eyes. Appears only in SECTION 1 and SECTION 18.
- FIRST BROTHER (eldest) — pink piglet, YELLOW dungarees, straw hat pushed back, droopy sleepy eyelids, always mid-yawn or mid-run-off-to-play. The biggest of the three.
- SECOND BROTHER (middle) — pink piglet, BLUE waistcoat, no hat, glances sideways at his big brother in almost every scene. Medium size.
- THIRD BROTHER (youngest) — pink piglet, GREEN dungarees rolled at the ankle, a smudge of clay on one cheek. Round soft cheeks and a small contented smile while he works — he ENJOYS building, he is never grim about it. The smallest of the three. Do NOT draw him stern, scowling, frowning, glaring or determined-looking. He is a cheerful little child, not a serious adult.
- WOLF — lean grey wolf, tall and a little shabby, threadbare rust-red scarf, hollow cheeks, long thin legs. Comic and hungry, never gruesome. He is a nuisance, not a monster.

RULES for every section:
(1) NO text anywhere — no letters, no Korean or English words, no numbers, no speech bubbles, no signature, no watermark, no caption strip. This is critical: an earlier attempt came back with English labels painted under the characters and had to be cropped out.
(2) Never draw the same character twice in one image. No duplicate figures.
(3) Only the characters named in that section appear. Do not add extra animals or people.
(4) The lower quarter of the frame must be fully painted, never left blank.
(5) Wholesome and child-friendly. No blood, no wounds, no fire touching anyone, no one being eaten. The wolf is never burned — he is only startled by hot air.
(6) Leave room for a caption band: keep faces and key action out of the bottom 18% of the frame where possible, since Korean text is laid over that strip in the app.
(7) FACES — read this twice. All three brothers are little children. Round soft cheeks, big friendly eyes, open gentle expressions. Never stern, never grim, never angry-looking, never adult, never heroic. Even in the tense scenes they must read as small worried children. An earlier attempt made the youngest brother look frightening; that is the single thing most important to avoid.

SECTION 1
Morning on a green hill. MOTHER PIG stands with her three sons in front of a small cottage, one hand raised as she speaks. The three brothers stand in a row facing her, listening — FIRST in yellow already looking off toward the meadow, SECOND in blue watching his big brother, THIRD in green looking up at his mother with a bright open face. Soft spring sky, scattered daisies.

SECTION 2
A lopsided straw house on the hillside, walls made of loose golden straw bundles. FIRST BROTHER is already running away from it toward the left edge, straw hat flying, grinning over his shoulder. A gust of wind bends one wall inward — a few straws lift into the air. Bright midday. FIRST BROTHER only.

SECTION 3
A house of woven brown sticks and branches beside a small tree. SECOND BROTHER in the blue waistcoat stands in front of it waving cheerfully toward the left, one hand cupped at his mouth as if calling to someone off-frame. The stick walls have visible gaps. Late afternoon light. SECOND BROTHER only.

SECTION 4
Lower slope of the hill, long low sun. THIRD BROTHER in green carries a red brick in both arms toward a half-built brick wall two courses high. A wooden tub of grey clay and a trowel sit beside it. He is smiling to himself as he works — cheeks round, eyes soft, clearly enjoying the job, humming rather than straining. Far away at the top of the hill two tiny figures in yellow and blue play, small and out of focus. He is alone in the foreground.

SECTION 5
The two older brothers walk past the half-built brick wall, laughing — FIRST in yellow with his head thrown back, SECOND in blue giggling behind a hoof. THIRD BROTHER stands at the wall having just set a brick down, looking at his own open palm, which is red and sore. His mouth droops a little and his brows lift in the middle — hurt and puzzled, NOT angry, NOT glaring. Warm afternoon. All three brothers.

SECTION 6
THIRD BROTHER sits alone on the ground beside the unfinished brick wall, knees drawn up, one hand resting flat on the top course of bricks as if stroking it. His head is tilted down but his eyes are on the bricks. His face is wistful and unsure, eyes soft and a little watery, on the edge of giving up — never stern, never hard. Long shadows, the sky beginning to turn amber. Nobody else in frame; the loneliness is the point.

SECTION 7
Evening, deep blue-and-amber sky. The finished brick cottage stands complete with a chimney and a thin curl of pale smoke rising from it. THIRD BROTHER stands in front with his back mostly to us, head tipped up, looking at his own house. A wide proud happy smile is visible in profile. Warm light glows in one window. THIRD BROTHER only.

SECTION 8
The WOLF steps out of a dark tree line at the bottom of the hill, thin and stooped, one paw against a trunk. His eyes are fixed uphill where the straw house sits small in the distance, lit by low sun. His ribs show faintly under the fur; the rust-red scarf hangs loose. WOLF only, with the straw house tiny in the background.

SECTION 9
Split composition at the straw house door. Outside, the WOLF leans close to the door, one paw raised mid-knock, the other cupped beside his muzzle as he speaks in a fake sweet voice — sly, eyebrows up. Inside, seen through a gap in the straw wall, FIRST BROTHER in yellow stands with his head tilted, puzzled and a bit suspicious. The door between them is the centre of the picture.

SECTION 10
The straw house bursts apart — straw flying outward like snow across the whole frame. The WOLF stands at the left with cheeks still puffed, having just blown. FIRST BROTHER sprints downhill to the right toward a stick house in the middle distance, arms flailing, hat gone, eyes wide with alarm. Motion lines and scattered straw everywhere.

SECTION 11
The stick house collapses — branches spraying out in all directions. The WOLF stands square in front of it, chest out, cheeks puffed. FIRST in yellow and SECOND in blue run out from the wreckage toward the right, holding hooves, both frightened but childlike. Dust and flying twigs. Late afternoon going dim.

SECTION 12
The brick cottage doorway, seen from inside-out at a slight angle. THIRD BROTHER in green has just thrown the door wide, one hoof still on the lifted wooden bar, his whole body leaning into the pull, his face open and urgent and kind. FIRST and SECOND are mid-stride crossing the threshold toward him. Warm firelight spills out past them into the blue evening. This is the emotional peak of the book — make the open door the brightest thing in the frame.

SECTION 13
The WOLF stands before the brick cottage with his cheeks enormously puffed, leaning back from the effort, one paw braced on his knee, looking dizzy and cross-eyed. The house does not move — only one window pane shows a faint wobble line. Three little pig faces (yellow, blue, green) peer out of the window together, round-cheeked and wide-eyed. Night, deep teal sky.

SECTION 14
Cutaway of the cottage: the WOLF is outside on the roof, climbing toward the chimney, seen in silhouette against the night sky. Inside the room below, THIRD BROTHER in green crouches at the fireplace pushing an armful of logs in; orange flames leap up. His face is lit warm and busy — quick and clever, not fierce. FIRST and SECOND watch from behind him. The chimney connects the two halves of the picture.

SECTION 15
On the roof at night. The WOLF is caught mid-recoil above the chimney — one hind leg still lifted over the opening, body arching backwards, eyes wide, mouth open in a yelp. A thick plume of hot steam and sparks bursts up out of the chimney into his face. He is NOT touching the fire and is NOT burned. Comic, not painful. Deep teal night sky.

SECTION 16
The WOLF is a small figure fleeing over the far hill toward the right edge, scarf streaming, legs a blur. In the foreground the brick cottage window glows warm and the three brothers (yellow, blue, green) are pressed side by side against the glass watching him go — relieved and soft-faced rather than laughing. Night.

SECTION 17
Inside the cottage, firelight only. FIRST BROTHER in yellow speaks with his head lowered and his hat held in both hooves; SECOND in blue stands beside him looking at the floor. THIRD BROTHER in green is guiding them by the shoulder toward the fireplace, where three small stools wait, his face gentle and welcoming. Very warm orange light, quiet mood, no big gestures.

SECTION 18
Bright spring morning on the hill. THREE brick cottages stand in a neat row, each with a smoking chimney. MOTHER PIG has just come up the path and is crouched with both arms wrapped around all three sons at once — FIRST in yellow, SECOND in blue, THIRD in green pressed together in her hug, all of them beaming. Green grass, blue sky, flowers. The warmest image in the book.
